Nicks Pickup Analyzer

A measurement suite for magnetic guitar/bass pickups. This tool needs access to your audio input device to read the signal coming from your pickup (via your audio interface).

How to connect: Plug your instrument (or a bare pickup with leads) into your audio interface. Passive measurements (spectrum, peak, impulse, polarity) just listen. Active tests (frequency response, saturation) play a reference tone out of your output device — route that to an exciter coil or small speaker placed near the pickup, or drive the pickup terminals through a resistor. Use headphones-free, low levels first.

Pluck a string and watch the spectrum. The peak frequency is the loudest partial; the spectral centroid is the "brightness" center of mass — a proxy for perceived tone. For an unamplified pickup, the resonant peak of the coil colours the harmonics.

A pickup is an RLC resonator: coil inductance, its self-capacitance + cable/pot load, and DC resistance. The stepped-sine sweep reveals the resonant peak (the "voice" of the pickup — bright single-coils peak high ~5–8 kHz, hot humbuckers lower ~2–4 kHz). Q describes how sharp/peaky that resonance is, and the HF slope shows how fast the top rolls off above it.

Why this matters: Real high-output pickup distortion is not the symmetric soft-clip most plugins use. As drive rises, the magnetic field around the pole pieces saturates on one polarity first — compressing one half of the waveform while the other stays linear. That asymmetry pumps out even harmonics (2nd, 4th) which sound "warm/amp-like", versus the odd-harmonic buzz of symmetric clipping. This mapper measures the +/− peak compression, the even-vs-odd harmonic balance, and the transfer-curve bend so you can see the true saturation shape — and, if you're modelling it, fit an asymmetric waveshaper instead of a tanh.

Caution: a passive pickup can't be electrically over-driven by this app directly. To exercise real magnetic saturation you must present a strong, increasing magnetic stimulus (a driven exciter coil / speaker coupling the ramped tone into the pickup, or a hot string signal). If you feed the pickup a small clean signal, you'll correctly see a straight transfer curve = no saturation.

Pickup Polarity Detector

Determines the electrical polarity (which lead is "hot" on a rising north-pole approach) and lets you compare two pickups for in-phase / out-of-phase pairing and hum-cancelling.

Arm, then bring a magnet/screwdriver toward the pole pieces and pull away — or tap sharply. We read the sign of the first voltage swing.

Polarity here is the electrical sign convention of the coil. For a single pickup it tells you which lead should be hot. Comparing two pickups: same electrical polarity + same magnetic polarity = in-phase and hum-adds; opposite electrical AND opposite magnetic = in-phase and hum-cancels (the classic humbucker / RWRP middle position). This app measures electrical polarity only — note the magnet orientation yourself.
